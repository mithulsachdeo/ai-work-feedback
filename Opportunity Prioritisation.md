## Note on 3, 5, 8 → 7
The team deliberated extensively on opportunities 3, 5, and 8, all of which converge on the same root cause: **no standard of "good" exists for how a non-technical professional should be using AI in their specific job**, so confidence stays untested until it's checked and it's too late to fix. Opportunity 7 is the synthesis of these three, scoped to the single persona (employed, mid-career, non-technical, daily AI user) with the tightest, most buildable core loop. It is the opportunity being carried forward as the MVP.

## MVP scoping decision: task type, not function
The behavioral pattern behind opportunity 7 (Silent Struggler / Overconfident Delegator) is **function-agnostic** — it shows up identically across Ops, Finance, HR, PM, and Sales personas in the research, all driven by the same underlying JTBD ("help me become capable enough that I don't feel professionally dependent on people who understand it better than I do"). Excluding functions to narrow scope would therefore both misrepresent the problem and needlessly shrink the reachable user pool right when 40–50 real users are needed fast.

What isn't function-agnostic is the *standard of "good"* itself — "good" for a Finance reconciliation and "good" for an HR job description are different rubrics, and building one per function is out of scope for an MVP timeline.

**Resolution: scope by artifact/task type instead of function.** The MVP evaluates one universal category — **AI-assisted written work product** (emails, summaries, decks, docs) — since it's the one task type every researched persona already uses AI for daily, regardless of department. This keeps the persona broad (recruit from any function) while keeping the "standard of good" tractable (one rubric, not N). Deeper artifact types (spreadsheets/dashboards, code, data analysis) are a v2 segmentation to make once usage data shows where real demand concentrates — not a v1 decision.
